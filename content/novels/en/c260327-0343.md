Only upon the station bell’s fourth chime did the awareness pierce me; quickening my stride, I
pitched myself into the carriage.

The sun was declining westward. A thick amber glare flooded the interior, washing over the features
of those gathered for the passage.

With a jolt the train tore into motion, and before my eyes every passenger yielded to the sudden
heave of momentum, rocking in unison.

People sat either with lids drawn over tired eyes, or else surrendered entirely to the luminous
miniature reliquaries cradled before them.

I cast my sight through the windowpane. Trees, dwellings, telegraph poles, highways, and fences
reeled past in relentless flight. What met my gaze were mere phantoms of green, russet, ash-gray,
soot-black, and chalk-white—squashed, splintered, kneaded into a single churning haze that dissolved
the very instant it took form.

Perhaps I have sunken far too deep.

All of it diaphanous, untethered—like the swimming blur that floods one’s vision in the wake of an
exhaustive bout of weeping, until the contours of that face have slipped beyond deciphering.

Along this traverse, what have I gleaned? What have I forfeited?

The occurrences of half a year past seem already washed out, as though subjected to the capricious
meddling of an inscrutable hand, or perhaps discarded by my own conscious will to unsee them.

What transpired in that year, after all?

I suppose I no longer retain it.

Ought I to retain it?

Perhaps not.

Another station chime cleaved through my reverie. I looked on as people surged frantically out of
the carriage as though fleeing a plague ground, only for a fresh throng to fling themselves inside
with equal desperation, as if scrambling toward some sanctuary of salvation.

The train heaved forward once more, its traction motors churning with an abrasive din; the landscape
before me, which had just regained its stillness, quickened anew until it curdled into a murky
paste.

How utterly I detest myself.

Only then did I register the amber dusk waning within the car. Once more I turned toward where the
sun was descending. The horizon was swallowing its final ember; soon it would slip once more beyond
my seeing.

I rested my palm against the glass, groping after some elusive substance, yet met only the icy
slickness of the pane.

Ought I to weep at this hour?

It seems an eternity since I last wept, as though the ducts had atrophied altogether—a notion
promptly refuted each time a yawn brings forth its moisture.

Perhaps I ought to weep; only then could I testify to my plight, my stance, my very self in all its
actuality.

Yet why is it that no weeping comes, struggle as I might? Surely the faculty was mine once?

Man’s very ingress into this world begins, does it not, with a wail. Where has it gone? I can locate
it no longer. I have somehow mislaid my lament.

Is this sorrow? Sorrow it must be.
