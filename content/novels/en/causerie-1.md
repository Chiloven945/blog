It is already the third deep autumn.

A mere, gentle step onto the fallen sycamore leaves awakens a crisp, rustling sonata underfoot. As a
breeze sweeps through, it stirs a rippling tide of russet and amber, whispering in concert with the
swaying boughs above, coaxing yet another scatter of foliage down to the earth. In the tranquil
depths of the grove, one catches only the faint, intermittent chorus of nameless insects. Peering
through the lacework of leaves, a pallid sky stretches out, layered in swathes of white washed with
faint, fleeting brushstrokes of azure. In the distance loom colossal steel beasts resembling jagged
mountain crests—it seems almost impossible that amidst the clamor of such a sprawling metropolis
lies this solitary sanctuary, cradling the pristine innocence of nature before it is entirely
consumed.

I pick up a fallen leaf and hold it toward the light; its hues of carmine and burnt orange flare
into brilliance, though scarcely dazzling. It is not, after all, a day of piercing sunshine, and my
eyes are spared any painful glare. Still, the distant blare of horns and the muffled dissonance of
the outside world filter in, disturbing this hard-won serenity. It is strange how effortlessly
people adapt to a life racing at the speed of light—the ceaseless grind of nine-to-five has long
become second nature, leaving none with even a spare breath to cast a passing glance at the world
around them. And I—how did I come to wander into this sycamore grove?

::scene-break
::

“Where do you think we go when we die?”

“I don’t know. Back to the soil, I suppose.”

A chuckle broke from his chest. He turned his gaze away from me, lifting it to the star-dusted
velvet of the night sky. Around us, the sycamores yielded, opening up their canopy above a small
clearing of forgotten origin, leaving our view unimpeded.

“I like to think we go up there,” he said, pointing heavenward.

My eyes followed his toward that deep indigo curtain, where stars clung in faint, shimmering
clusters—like a painter’s spontaneous flecks of white pigment, an inspired flourish that brought the
whole canvas alive, yet so artfully placed that any deliberate mimicry would fall pale. Perhaps this
was the siren call of that distant cosmos: an awe born of the unknown, one that commands our wonder
yet keeps us tethered to the ground.

“When my time comes, I’ll surely become one of those stars, looking down on you from the edge of the
sky…”

Resting his chin on his knees, he gazed longing into the far reaches of the night. I looked at him,
an unspoken foreboding brushing against my heart, leaving an ache that tasted bitter and cold.

“Don't say such morbid things.”

“Alright, alright…”

::scene-break
::

The musky scent of decaying leaves and damp earth never nauseated me; rather, it held me spellbound.
Whenever those shifting, earthy notes hung in the air, my thoughts would drift back to that
star-strewn night. It felt as diaphanous and elusive as a dream, yet etched with the indelible sting
of reality. How I cherished those quiet strolls with him through the sycamores—listening to
birdsong, drenched in the dappled sunlight filtering through the canopy, breathing in the crisp,
mingling aromas of the woods, losing ourselves in conversation beneath the leaf-shadows… until the
day I realized he was gone forever.

A nameless illness claimed his life—merciless, incurable, creeping in without a sound, and stealing
away in stifled silence. Cruelest of all was the sheer absurdity of an unyielding fate, leaving me
with nothing save a cold, hollow shell resting in the mortuary. Yet curiously, no tears came. I
remained eerily composed, as though numbed to the marrow by the blow. I tried, even forced myself to
weep, but to no avail; all I could muster was a face contorted in agony. I stared into those
forever-closed eyes and ran my fingers along his cheek one last time—it was freezing. I longed
desperately to warm him once more, but the lingering thread of sanity within whispered coldly: Give
it up. It’s impossible.

It was like stumbling blindly through an abyss of nothingness, headless and disoriented, clutching
only a frail thread of reason against the verge of howling madness. In the end, my composure
shattered, my footing dissolved; I stood like a dancer stripping away every defense before an
invisible foe, my vulnerabilities exposed, naked and complete. Grief became an affliction. Night
after night, dream after dream, his silhouette returned to me—a fragile, haunting beauty, only to
dissolve like a soap bubble at dawn, leaving nothing behind.

In the end, all he became was a square metal box, neither heavy nor light, weighing precisely four
pounds.

::scene-break
::

“My favorite place? Probably the sea.”

“Oh? Why’s that?”

I took a sip of the half-empty glass of orange juice in my hand; the ice cubes clinked sharply
against the glass.

“Well, you know… I grew up in the mountains.”

My eyes followed his to the distant horizon where deep navy and soft cyan merged at the edge of the
world, touched by an elusive shimmer of white whose glare forced me to look away.

::scene-break
::

The traffic lights cycled mechanically through red, yellow, and green; the sidewalks stretched out
in hushed silence; old streetlamps cast a hazy amber glow; along the embankment, moss crept across
stones washed smooth by the river; in the distance, sailboats swayed languidly against nearly
abandoned docks… Before I knew it, my car had carried me to the harbor. Here, where river meets sea,
lies the bustling lifeline of the city’s trade. Across the waters, tugs and freighters occasionally
glided by, their low horns reverberating with a deafening thrum.

It was the final breath of dusk. The water, typically dull and pale, caught the embers of sunset and
turned into molten, gilded orange. The softness of the sand yielded beneath my soles; the closer I
drew to the tide line, the more pungent the briny breeze became. The chilled sea finally grazed my
fingers, teasing playfully through the spaces between them, ebbing and flowing with the tide. My
gaze finally settled upon the metal box clutched against my chest, reflecting the dying embers of
the twilight and the face I had known so intimately every single day.

“Why didn't you tell me?”

Drip, tap. One drop, then two, pooled atop the lid as the world before me blurred into water. I had
only feared losing him—the one I had searched so long to find, the one who shared every sorrow and
joy, the one beside whom I could sleep in peace… the one who was there, without fail, day after day.
Yet lost him I had. Could I not even keep this box as a token to hold onto?

“Why are you still such a crybaby?”

His voice seemed to brush past my ear, like a spectral hand gently tousling my hair, caressing my
cheek, grazing the sharp points of my canines.

::scene-break
::

“I’ll be watching you from the edge of the sky…”

“Don't say such morbid things.”

“Alright…”

He turned toward me, wearing that familiar, unchanging smile.

“That way, whenever you feel lonely, you only have to look up—and there I’ll be.”

Silence was the only answer I could offer.

::scene-break
::

I looked up: the sky that had once glowed with liquid gold had surrendered to a mantle of bruised
indigo and violet as the curtain of night fell in silence. The stars reemerged, seemingly more
abundant this time, like countless unblinking eyes watching over the earth, rendering everything
beneath their celestial brilliance all the more humble and dim. In this fleeting expanse between
heaven and earth, there was only the vast sea of stars to keep me company.

::scene-break
::

“Then how am I supposed to know which one is you?”

“Silly, the brightest one, of course!”

He pointed into the vast expanse, but amidst the radiant sprawl of the Milky Way, how could I ever
trace his path?

“The brightest one to you.”

::scene-break
::

A horn hummed from a distant freighter, while the nearby lighthouse cut its first beam into the
emerging dark. Turning to the heavens, I saw it: the brightest star pulsing gently, as if waving,
keeping vigil over my loneliness in this moment and every moment yet to come. We had met under a
star-strewn sky in early autumn, and parted under another as autumn drew to a close. He was like an
elusive shadow, slipping past in an ephemeral encounter—yet he had plucked the chords of my heart,
leaving me utterly spellbound.

The fine dust of calcium, phosphorus, oxygen, and carbon finally dissolved into the sea, drifting
into the currents alongside that single sycamore leaf.

::scene-break
::

I returned to the sycamore grove.

With the return of spring, the leaves of yesteryear had long melded into the loam. Upon the
branches, tender green buds unfurled, swaying gently in the breeze alongside clusters of unripe
fruit. There were no withered leaves this time, no drone of insects, no lingering chill, and no him.
Yet in their place, I found tender shoots, the return of birdsong, the stirrings of life, and an
all-embracing warmth.

*“Weep not for my parting, for I have never left your side.”*
